# Niche: Heritage Restoration Contractors (UK)

**Chosen niche:** owner-run UK firms that repair and restore old buildings using traditional crafts: stonemasonry, lime plastering and repointing, heritage roofing (slate, stone, clay tile, thatch), leadwork, and traditional joinery such as sash windows.

**One-line positioning:** *"I write for heritage craftspeople. You send voice notes and site photos from your phone; I turn them into posts that architects, surveyors and owners of old buildings actually read."*

This is a hypothesis backed by desk research, not yet by conversations. Validate it with the steps in section 6 before rebuilding everything around it.

---

## 1. Why a rare niche

Generic LinkedIn ghostwriting for founders and executives is crowded. One industry report counts 200+ agencies and says broad-topic rates are being pushed down. Specialists command higher rates and grow by referral. A niche where the work is physical and human, the owners are not online people, and nobody else writes for them gives you three advantages:

1. **Almost no competition.** I found no ghostwriters serving heritage contractors. The marketing agencies in adjacent trades sell ads and websites, not the owner's own voice.
2. **Content that writes itself.** Every job has a story: a 300-year-old building, a failed cement repair being undone, a technique passed down, a find behind a wall. This material is visual and specific, and people share it.
3. **Expensive jobs.** Heritage repair contracts are large, so one extra project from an architect's referral pays for a year of your service.

**Why the others ranked lower:**
- **Funeral directors.** Specialist agencies already serve them, and the content is mostly obituaries and sensitive material.
- **Home care.** Crowded with agencies, and the content would mainly be recruitment.
- **Executive search.** Recruiters already live on LinkedIn and many write their own posts.
- **Commercial mediators** are the best backup. Their work comes from lawyer referrals, and LinkedIn keeps them in front of lawyers between referrals.
- **Bespoke makers** are the second backup. They sell mostly through Instagram, and a big commission is worth £1,000–£16,000.

## 3. Evidence for the chosen niche

- **Large, old building stock.** In England, 21% of homes and 32% of non-domestic buildings were built before 1919 (Historic England).
- **A real economy.** Heritage-related construction produced £7.1bn GVA and employed over 100,000 people in England, according to 2018 data from the 2019 *Heritage and the Economy* report. This is dated, so check for newer figures.
- **Skills shortage.** Historic England's 2024 skills-needs analysis says the industry lacks the skills to repair and retrofit traditional buildings. Firms with real skills therefore have an edge, but only if specifiers know about them.
- **Small, identifiable specialist pool.** Roughly 90% of firms that repair historic buildings call themselves general builders, and an older IHBC survey found fewer than 250 accredited conservation firms. That makes the accredited specialists easy to find and easy to differentiate.
- **Buyers who check credibility.** Architects and specifiers lean on registers such as the NFRC Heritage Roofing Register and the Lead Contractors Association's graded directory. Firms already compete on visible track record, which posts can show week after week.

## 4. Ideal client profile

- Owner-led firm, 3–40 people, in business 10+ years, with at least one accreditation or register listing, or a portfolio of listed-building work.
- The owner is a craftsperson first and posts rarely, if at all. They probably have a dated website and many site photos on their phone.
- They want more of the right jobs (larger, better-paid, specified by architects), or apprentices and skilled staff.
- They are based in the UK and paid in GBP. Ireland, with its own Register of Heritage Contractors, is a second market.

**Who they want to reach**

| Audience | Where they are |
|----------|----------------|
| Conservation architects and surveyors | LinkedIn |
| Local authority conservation officers | LinkedIn |
| Estate and property managers, church and diocese committees, heritage trusts | LinkedIn, Facebook |
| Owners of listed and period homes | Instagram, Facebook, Google search (Google Business Profile) |
| Apprentices and skilled tradespeople | Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts |

This is why the service has to cover several platforms, and why the new social optimiser exists.

## 5. The offer, shaped for this niche

**Low-tech for the client**
- They don't need a laptop. Each week they send 2–5 WhatsApp voice notes (*"what we did this week and why"*) plus site photos.
- One 30-minute **phone** call a month replaces video interviews.
- They approve drafts by WhatsApp reply: "ok" or "change X".

**What you deliver**
- LinkedIn posts in the owner's voice, aimed at specifiers.
- Each approved post adapted with the social optimiser for Instagram, Facebook, Google Business Profile and X, plus short-video scripts for Reels, TikTok and Shorts.
- A monthly "project story": one longer piece about a finished job, for their website or a trade publication.
- Scheduling on every platform.

**Proof to build first:** write 5 sample posts from public material, such as firms' own project pages and Building Conservation Directory articles. Make sure every technical term is right; see `heritage-primer.md`.

Price in GBP. Use the `pricing` / `hormozi-framework` skills to set tiers. This doc doesn't set prices for you.

## 6. Validate before committing (2–3 weeks)

1. **Build a list of 60 firms** from the NFRC Heritage Roofing Register, the Lead Contractors Association directory, the Building Conservation Directory, and IHBC specialist registers. Log each one in the tracker's Pipeline tab as a prospect, with a hook taken from one of their real projects.
2. **Talk to 10 owners by phone or email** before selling anything. Ask: How do you win your best jobs now? Who specifies you? Have you tried posting? What stopped you? What's one more architect-specified job worth?
3. **Offer 3 free samples:** 3 posts each, made from their own project pages.
4. **Go/no-go:** if 3 or more of the 10 say they'd pay, and at least 1 signs a founding-client deal within 4 weeks, commit. If not, run the same test on commercial mediators.

## 7. Risks

| Risk | Handling |
|------|----------|
| Technical mistakes, such as calling lime "cement" | Use `heritage-primer.md`. The owner approves every post. Never add a technical claim the owner didn't make. |
| UK clients wary of an overseas writer | Lead with craft knowledge and samples, and be upfront about where you are. GMT+1 (Lagos) overlaps UK working hours. |
| Owners say "social media isn't for us" | Point to architects and conservation officers on LinkedIn, and to Google Business Profile for local search. Show them a competitor's work being seen. |
| Small market | It's enough for a solo service. If you outgrow it, expand to Ireland, to adjacent crafts (stained glass, blacksmithing, thatching), or to the mediator niche. |
| Facts in this doc are dated | The 2018/2019 figures and the older IHBC survey are marked as such. Recheck with Historic England before quoting them to clients. |
