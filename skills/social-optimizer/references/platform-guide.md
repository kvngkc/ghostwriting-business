# Platform Guide

Hard limits live in `platforms.json`, which the checker script reads. This file is about what works on each platform. Treat the advice as starting points, and use the client's own results once you have them.

## LinkedIn (the master post)
- **Audience:** professionals and specifiers (for the heritage niche: architects, surveyors, conservation officers).
- **Shape:** a hook in the first two lines (the rest hides behind "see more"), short paragraphs, one idea, one story.
- **Ending:** a question or a plain takeaway. 0–3 hashtags.
- **Avoid:** links in the post body if the client finds they lower reach. Put the link in the first comment instead.

## X
- 280 characters per post unless the client pays for Premium.
- One sharp claim from the post, or a thread of 3–6 posts. Each post in a thread has to make sense on its own.
- 0–2 hashtags. Write as a person would talk, not as a headline.

## Threads
- Up to 500 characters. Conversational, a bit looser than LinkedIn.
- Lead with the human moment rather than the lesson. At most one topic tag.

## Instagram (feed post or carousel)
- The image does the work and the caption supports it. Specify which photo goes first.
- **Caption:** the first line hooks (about the first 125 characters show before "more"), then the story, then a soft call to action ("Restoring a listed building? Link in bio.").
- **Carousel outline:** 5–8 slides. Slide 1 is the hook over the best photo, slides 2–6 are the process, and the last slide is the result plus the call to action. Before/after works very well for restoration.
- Up to 5 specific hashtags (e.g. #limemortar, #listedbuilding, #stonemasonry), never generic ones like #love.
- Alt text for each image: describe what is in it.

## Facebook
- **Audience:** local community and older homeowners. Warmer and more local than LinkedIn: name the town, thank the client, mention the team by first name if they agree.
- Keep it short. Long posts get cut off early. One photo or a small album.

## Google Business Profile ("Updates" posts)
- **Audience:** people searching locally for the service ("lime plastering near me").
- **Shape:** plain and factual. What was done, where (town or area, not the street, unless the owner agreed), and the service name in normal words.
- No hashtags. Don't put phone numbers in the text; use the post's button (Call / Learn more).
- One clear photo. Post weekly to keep the profile active.

## Short video (Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts)
- Write a **script for the owner to read on site**, 20–60 seconds, in their own words, with a shot list:
  1. Hook (0–3 seconds): show the most surprising thing ("This wall was killed by cement").
  2. What we're doing and why (3–4 short lines).
  3. The result or reveal.
  4. One-line close ("Old buildings need lime, not cement.").
- Short spoken sentences. If a sentence is hard to say out loud, rewrite it.
- **Shot list:** close-up of hands and tools, a wide shot of the building, before and after.
- Caption: one line plus 3–5 hashtags. YouTube Shorts also needs a title under 100 characters.

## Adapting across platforms

- **Same truth everywhere.** Every version uses only facts in the approved master post or the client's material. Adapting changes the form, never the facts.
- **Same voice, different register.** Keep the client's words and phrases, and change length and warmth per platform.
- **Lead with what each platform rewards.** LinkedIn leads with the lesson, Instagram with the image, Facebook with the local/human angle, Google with the service and place, and video with the surprise.
- **Don't publish the same text everywhere.** Copy-paste across platforms reads as automated and wastes the strengths of each one.
