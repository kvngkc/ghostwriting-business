# Client Interview Question Bank

Record every interview (with the client's permission) and transcribe it. Aim for 30 to 45 minutes. You do not need every question; pick 12 to 15 and follow the interesting answers. Ask for specifics every time: "Can you give me an example?", "What happened next?", "What was the number?"

## First interview (onboarding)

**Who they are and who they talk to**
1. In one or two sentences, what do you do and who do you do it for?
2. Who do you most want reading your posts? Describe one real person.
3. What do you want those people to think or do after following you for six months?

**Origin and turning points**
4. How did you end up doing this work? What was the moment you decided?
5. What is the worst professional mistake you have made, and what did it teach you?
6. Tell me about a client or project that changed how you work.

**Opinions**
7. What does most of your industry get wrong?
8. What common advice in your field do you disagree with? Why?
9. What is something you believed five years ago that you no longer believe?
10. What question do prospects ask you most? How do you answer it?

**Proof and specifics**
11. What results are you proudest of? Which numbers can I use publicly?
12. Which client stories can be told, named or anonymised? Which are off limits?

**Voice and boundaries**
13. Whose writing or speaking do you like? What do you like about it?
14. Which words or phrases do you hate seeing in posts?
15. Are there topics you never want to post about (politics, family, competitors, money)?
16. How do you feel about emojis, hashtags, swearing, humour?
17. Do you want posts to mention that you work with a writer? (Record the answer.)

## Monthly interview (content refill, 30 minutes)

1. What happened in your work this month that surprised you?
2. What did a client or prospect say that stuck with you?
3. What did you disagree with this month, online or in a meeting?
4. What decision did you make, and what were the options?
5. What are you working on next month that people should hear about?
6. Which posts from last month felt most like you? Which felt least like you?
7. Anything to avoid this month (launches, legal, sensitive news)?

## Tips

- Let them talk. Their natural phrasing is the raw material for the voice profile.
- When they say something vivid, ask them to say it again in their own words; quote it exactly.
- Note timestamps for good stories so posts can be traced back to the source.
