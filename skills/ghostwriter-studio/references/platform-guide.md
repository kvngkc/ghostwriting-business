# Platform Guide

Hard limits live in `platforms.json`, which the checker script reads. This file is about what works on each platform. Treat the advice as starting points: the writer's playbook and the client's profile win over anything here (if the client never uses hashtags or calls to action, neither do their versions), and the client's own results win once you have them. Niche packs may add platform notes for their field.

## LinkedIn (the master post)
- **Audience:** professionals, buyers and peers in the client's field.
- **Shape:** a hook in the first two lines (the rest hides behind "see more"), short paragraphs, one idea, one story.
- **Ending:** a question or a plain takeaway. 0–3 hashtags.
- **Avoid:** links in the post body if the client finds they lower reach. Put the link in the first comment instead.

## X
- 280 characters per post unless the client pays for Premium.
- One sharp claim from the post, or a thread of 3–6 posts. Each post in a thread has to make sense on its own.
- 0–2 hashtags. Write as a person would talk, not as a headline.

## Threads
- Up to 500 characters. Conversational, a bit looser than LinkedIn.
- Lead with the human moment rather than the lesson. At most one topic tag.

## Instagram (feed post or carousel)
- The image does the work and the caption supports it. Specify which photo goes first, and ask the client for it rather than assuming one exists.
- **Caption:** the first line hooks (about the first 125 characters show before "more"), then the story, then an optional soft call to action ("Link in bio.") if the client uses them.
- **Carousel outline:** 5–8 slides. Slide 1 is the hook over the best photo, the middle slides tell the story or process, and the last slide is the result (plus a call to action if the client uses them). Before/after works well wherever there's a visible change.
- Up to 5 specific hashtags for the client's field, never generic ones like #love.
- Alt text for each image: describe what is in it.

## Facebook
- **Audience:** local community and existing customers. Warmer and more local than LinkedIn: name the town, thank the client, mention the team by first name if they agree.
- Keep it short. Long posts get cut off early. One photo or a small album.

## Google Business Profile ("Updates" posts)
- **Audience:** people searching locally for the service ("<service> near me").
- **Shape:** plain and factual. What was done, where (town or area, never a customer's address unless they agreed), and the service name in normal words.
- No hashtags. Don't put phone numbers in the text; use the post's button (Call / Learn more).
- One clear photo. Post weekly to keep the profile active.

## Short video (Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts)
- Write a **script for the client to read to camera**, 20–60 seconds, in their own words, with a shot list:
  1. Hook (0–3 seconds): the most surprising fact or sight from the story.
  2. What happened and why (3–4 short lines).
  3. The result or reveal.
  4. One-line close, ideally a line the client already says.
- Short spoken sentences. If a sentence is hard to say out loud, rewrite it. A single short story can run under 20 seconds; don't pad it.
- **Shot list:** the client to camera, close-ups of the thing the story is about, before and after if there is one. Check that nothing on screen identifies a customer who hasn't agreed.
- Caption: one line plus 3–5 hashtags (or none, if the playbook says so). YouTube Shorts also needs a title under 100 characters.
- No platform named: write one script and check it against `instagram_reels`, `tiktok` and `youtube_shorts`.

## Adapting across platforms

- **Same truth everywhere.** Every version uses only facts in the master post or the client's material. Adapting changes the form, never the facts.
- **Same voice, different register.** Keep the client's words and phrases, and change length and warmth per platform.
- **Lead with what each platform rewards.** LinkedIn leads with the lesson, Instagram with the image, Facebook with the local/human angle, Google with the service and place, and video with the surprise.
- **Don't publish the same text everywhere.** Copy-paste across platforms reads as automated and wastes the strengths of each one.
