# Platform Guide

Hard limits live in `platforms.json`, which the checker script reads. This file is about what tends to work on each platform. These are defaults: the writer's playbook and the client's own habits override them (for example, if the playbook bans hashtags, use none anywhere). Use the client's own results once you have them.

## LinkedIn (usually the master post)
- **Audience:** the client's professional buyers, peers and referrers.
- **Shape:** a hook in the first two lines (the rest hides behind "see more"), short paragraphs, one idea, one story.
- **Ending:** whatever the playbook says. Default: a plain takeaway or a question the audience can answer. 0–3 specific hashtags.
- **Avoid:** links in the post body if the client finds they lower reach. Put the link in the first comment instead.

## X
- 280 characters per post unless the client pays for Premium.
- One sharp claim from the post, or a thread of 3–6 posts. Each post in a thread has to make sense on its own. Only thread when the story genuinely needs it.
- 0–2 hashtags. Write as a person would talk, not as a headline.

## Threads
- Up to 500 characters. Conversational, a bit looser than LinkedIn.
- Lead with the human moment rather than the lesson. At most one topic tag.

## Instagram (feed post or carousel)
- The image does the work and the caption supports it. Say which photo goes first. If no photo exists, ask for one (`[ASK CLIENT]`) or suggest a text carousel.
- **Caption:** the first line hooks (about the first 125 characters show before "more"), then the story. Add a soft call to action only if the client uses them.
- **Carousel outline:** 5–8 slides. Slide 1 is the hook, the middle slides tell the story or steps, and the last slide is the payoff.
- Default up to 5 specific hashtags, never generic ones like #love, unless the playbook or client says none.
- Alt text for each image: describe what is in it.
- Never show anything that identifies a person or business the client hasn't cleared (signage, faces, documents).

## Facebook
- **Audience:** often local and community-minded. Warmer and more personal than LinkedIn: team members by first name if they agree, thanks to customers who agreed to be named.
- Keep it short, because long posts get cut off early. One photo or a small album.

## Google Business Profile ("Updates" posts)
- **Audience:** people searching locally for the client's service.
- **Shape:** plain and factual. What was done, roughly where (area, not address, unless cleared), and the service name in normal words.
- No hashtags. Don't put phone numbers in the text; use the post's button (Call / Learn more).
- One clear photo.

## Short video (Reels by default; TikTok and YouTube Shorts if named)
- Write a **script the client can say to camera**, 20–60 seconds, in their own words, with a shot list:
  1. Hook (0–3 seconds): the most surprising fact or moment from the post.
  2. What happened and why (3–4 short spoken lines).
  3. The result or reveal.
  4. A one-line close, ideally a phrase the client really uses.
- Short spoken sentences. If a sentence is hard to say out loud, rewrite it.
- **Shot list:** what to film (the client talking, the place or object in the story, before/after if there is one).
- Caption: one line (hashtags per the playbook). YouTube Shorts also needs a title under 100 characters.

## Adapting across platforms

- **Same truth everywhere.** Every version uses only facts in the master post or the client's material. Adapting changes the form, never the facts.
- **Same voice, different register.** Keep the client's words and phrases, and change length and warmth per platform.
- **Lead with what each platform rewards.** LinkedIn leads with the lesson, Instagram with the image, Facebook with the human angle, Google with the service, and video with the surprise.
- **Don't publish the same text everywhere.** Copy-paste across platforms reads as automated and wastes the strengths of each one.
